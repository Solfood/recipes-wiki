# Guinness Beef Stew
*(Oven Braise • Leek • Carrot • Parsnip • Worcestershire)*

!!! info "Recipe Details"
    *   **Yield:** 4–5 servings
    *   **Cook Time:** ~3h (2h braise + 30–40m with vegetables)
    *   **Oven:** 140°C (285°F)

## Ingredients

### Beef & Searing
- [ ] **1.2 kg** beef chuck (or tri-tip, see [Cut Options](#cut-options)), cut into large chunks
- [ ] **Neutral high-smoke-point oil**, as needed
- [ ] **Salt and black pepper**, to taste

### Vegetables
- [ ] **275 g** leeks, cleaned and thinly sliced
- [ ] **225 g** carrots, large chunks
- [ ] **200 g** parsnips, large chunks

### Braising Liquid & Seasoning
- [ ] **440 ml** Guinness Original
- [ ] **20 ml** Worcestershire sauce
- [ ] *(Optional)* **Unsalted chicken stock**, as needed
- [ ] *(Optional)* **1** bay leaf
- [ ] *(Optional)* **1 small** pitted prune (~10 g)

## Method

### 1. Sear the Beef
Heat a thin layer of neutral oil in a heavy casserole. Season the beef and sear in batches until deeply browned. Allow the pot to regain temperature between batches. Remove the beef and reserve its juices.

### 2. Brown the Vegetables
Add the carrots and parsnips to the same pot. Brown their surfaces in the rendered beef fat, then remove and set aside. They will return towards the end of the braise to preserve their texture.

### 3. Cook the Leeks
Remove excess fat if necessary. Add the sliced leeks with a little salt and cook gently until softened. Allow their moisture to release the fond, then continue cooking until most of that moisture has evaporated.

### 4. Build the Seasoning
Add the Worcestershire sauce and stir briefly. Its savoury, acidic and dark sweet notes help complement the Guinness.

### 5. Deglaze with Guinness
Add some of the Guinness, scraping the bottom of the casserole to dissolve the remaining fond.

### 6. Assemble the Braise
Return the beef and its resting juices to the pot. Add the remaining Guinness and, if needed, enough unsalted chicken stock to achieve the desired liquid level. Add the optional bay leaf and prune.

### 7. Braise
Bring to a gentle simmer, cover and cook in a **140°C** oven for **2 hours**.

!!! warning "Using tri-tip?"
    Start checking at **1½ hours**. Tri-tip is leaner than chuck, so it goes from tender to dry and stringy faster. When a chunk is nearly tender, move on to step 8.

### 8. Return the Vegetables
Add the reserved carrots and parsnips. Bring the pot back to a boil. Return to the oven for another **30–40 minutes**, until the beef is tender and the vegetables are cooked. The lid can be removed during this stage if a little reduction is needed.

### 9. Finish the Gravy
Taste and adjust the seasoning. If the gravy needs further reduction, remove some to a separate saucepan and reduce it independently so the beef and vegetables do not overcook.

## Cut Options

Any cut with plenty of connective tissue works well in a long, gentle braise. Leaner cuts work too, but they need a shorter braise and a closer eye.

| Cut | How it braises | Adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| **Chuck** (roast, eye, or flat iron trimmings) | The standard choice: marbled and full of collagen, so it turns silky | Use the recipe as written |
| **Boneless short rib** | Richest option, very forgiving | Same timing. Skim extra fat in step 9 |
| **Brisket point** (deckle) | Rich and beefy, holds its shape | Same timing, or up to 30m longer |
| **Beef cheeks** | Turns very gelatinous and spoon-tender | Add 30–60m to the braise |
| **Shank** (bone-in or boneless) | Lots of collagen, very rich gravy | Add 30–60m. Bone-in adds body |
| **Tri-tip** | Beefy and tender, but lean, so less gelatinous | Cut into larger chunks (~5 cm), don't trim all the fat cap, check from 1½h |
| **Bottom round / rump** | Lean, can turn dry and stringy | Same as tri-tip. Add stock so the beef stays submerged |

!!! tip "Chef's Notes"
    *   **Tri-tip:** the gravy will have less natural body than with chuck. Reduce it separately in step 9, or add a little more stock early and reduce it harder at the end.
    *   **Don't use** steak cuts like sirloin, strip, or tenderloin. They have no collagen to break down and will just dry out.
    *   The **prune** dissolves into the gravy for a background dark sweetness. You won't taste it as fruit.
